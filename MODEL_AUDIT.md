# Model and recommendation audit — 2026-11-03

## Specific stocks and funds

The questionnaire screens a fixed universe of 13 listed US stocks and funds: AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, JPM, JNJ, XOM, SPY, QQQ, GLD, SLV, DBC, TLT and SHY. It requests adjusted daily history from 2014-01-01 using explicit date bounds (Yahoo range=max can silently return monthly bars), validates the daily interval, and uses Yahoo Finance's chart endpoint. It caches prices for six hours, excludes future bars and today's US bar before 22:00 UTC, and rejects series whose latest price is over seven calendar days old. Each series needs at least 210 complete daily observations; at least ten fresh series are required before returning ideas. The base score ranks three-month momentum and distance above the 200-day mean, then penalizes volatility according to risk capacity. A horizon under one year restricts ideas to SHY. Risk filters do not guarantee a drawdown limit. Commodity choices are exchange-traded funds, not physical commodities.

The earlier price endpoint check returned 2026-11-02 prices for both specific-stock and conservative questionnaires. The recorded older, price-only diagnostic in `validate_market_recs.py` showed 24.9% annualized gross return and -19.3% maximum drawdown from 2023-01-01 through 2026-11-02, versus SPY at 20.9% and -18.8%. It does **not** validate the new news/history overlay or establish a forecasting edge. The universe was chosen with knowledge of surviving current listings, the period was examined retrospectively, and costs, taxes and slippage are omitted. The app labels the output as research suggestions, not machine-learning return predictions or instructions to buy.

Yahoo's chart endpoint is an external dependency. Insufficient current price coverage produces no ideas rather than invented numbers. Before a public launch, add a licensed market-data provider, a delisted-security history, fees, and an untouched forward test.

## News and historical-event evidence

`news_evidence.py` makes on-demand public Google News RSS requests using fixed company, sector and macro queries; it does not send profile text. News is cached for 20 minutes. Only dated, linked headlines within the preceding 72 hours are eligible, and future dates, unsafe links and duplicates are rejected. Partial or failed feeds are disclosed. The code distinguishes company-specific headlines from sector or macro context, and excludes hypothetical, negated or conflicting event language from historical support. Keyword matches are not fact verification, and two publisher labels do not prove that reporting is independent.

The curated library contains seven dated events with primary-source links: Federal Reserve rate cuts in July 2019, March 2020 and September 2024; rate increases in December 2015 and March 2022; the FDIC's March 2023 Silicon Valley Bank closure; and the February 2022 invasion of Ukraine documented by the UN. These selected examples are not a complete or unbiased sample of market events. A headline with no supported topic match produces no forced historical analogy.

`market_evidence.py` computes each candidate instrument's own adjusted return from the event-day closing price, or the next trading-session close if the market was closed, to five and 20 sessions later. SPY is measured over exactly the same start/end dates. Actual dates and missing windows are returned; uncovered windows are omitted without endpoint extrapolation. Because the baseline is the event-day close, the measurement generally excludes that day's initial response. These are subsequent-return associations, not causal estimates, executable backtests or predictions. Unrelated events and market conditions can explain the observed returns.

The experimental overlay can change a score only with at least two covered, non-overlapping historical event windows for the same topic, separated by at least 45 calendar days and checked against actual window endpoints, plus eligible current headlines from at least two distinct publishers. It uses 10% of the median 20-session excess return versus SPY, capped at plus or minus one ranking point. If support is insufficient, the benchmark is missing, or eligible historical themes disagree in direction, the adjustment is zero. Separation avoids overlapping samples but does not establish statistical independence. This small-sample rule and its cap have not been validated for predictive performance.

`POST /api/recommend` exposes the evidence in its `market` response: dated market behavior, publisher headlines, primary event links, returns and comparison dates, missing-data status, separate price/risk and news/history score components, and justifications/cautions. The interface and explanations support English and Polish; source text remains in its original language. Prices, returns and score adjustments are computed in code, not supplied by a language model. No immutable archive records the news that was actually available at each past decision time. A valid forward evaluation of the overlay, including retrieval timestamps, costs, an unchanged selection rule and a suitable benchmark, remains outstanding.

The deterministic `test_news_evidence.py` and `test_market_evidence.py` suites check freshness, deduplication, speculative-event exclusions, source handling, exact trading-session windows, missing benchmark dates, evidence gates, score caps, conflicting themes and outage behavior. `test_historical_analog.py` checks that the separate legacy panel can abstain, refuses stale/future source evidence and uses only covered, dated strategy-curve observations. Those sparse strategy changes are approximate context and must not be treated as individual-stock evidence. `test_market_route.py` also verifies English/Polish API explanations and price-outage behavior. All 49 tests passed on 2026-11-03, along with JavaScript syntax and escaped evidence rendering checks. Public live checks returned 13 current price series and 151 recent headlines; QQQ, GLD and XOM covered all seven event windows. These tests verify implementation behavior, not investment returns or forecasting accuracy.

## Crypto volatility model

The bundled July model report was reproduced exactly from its cached data: neural-network AUC 0.7008. Coinbase prices were then refreshed through 2026-11-02. Ten days before each train/validation/test boundary were removed so forward volatility labels cannot use the following period's prices. The retrained selected neural net reached AUC 0.6666 on 11,810 later-period examples; random forest reached 0.6810 and a simple persistence baseline 0.6323. The saved model and report now match these results. The model estimates only crypto turbulence, not the direction of prices or returns for stocks and funds. Live predictions are withheld if input prices are over seven days old. MATIC's series ended in 2025 and remains only historical training data.
